Scope and reading guide

This review covers developments first announced, published, demonstrated, or materially updated during the seven most recently completed calendar days. It prioritizes primary sources, uses independent reporting to test commercial claims, and separates observed facts from interpretation.

Evidence labels: Fact means directly supported by a cited source; company-reported means the source is the vendor and independent verification was not found; interpretation is this review’s synthesis; prediction is forward-looking; and uncertainty identifies a material evidence gap. IPAI is treated as an applied-AI institution concerned with research translation, evaluation, capability building, industrial adoption, trustworthy deployment, and ecosystem strategy.

1. Executive summary

The week’s most consequential change was not a single model release. It was the hardening of the operating environment around advanced AI: privacy-preserving monitoring, cyber-capability gates, agentic retrieval, cheaper frontier inference, and unprecedented commitments to compute capacity. In parallel, robotics discourse moved away from athletic demonstrations and toward the less glamorous constraints that determine commercial value—task reliability, supervision, data capture, validation, and total cost.

  1. Frontier AI safety became a production-systems problem. OpenAI said preliminary evidence indicates its forthcoming Astra model may meet its “Critical” cybersecurity threshold. It disclosed a two-week pause in reinforcement-learning training, continued suspension of its largest planned frontier RL run, stronger workload and network isolation, and multistage monitoring whose current compute overhead it estimates at roughly 20%. These are company disclosures, not an independent capability assessment, but they are operationally specific and therefore significant.[1]
  2. Privacy and longitudinal safeguards are being engineered together. OpenAI’s preview of Private Safety Processing aims to identify risky patterns across related interactions while maintaining Zero Data Retention: content remains under customer control or is encrypted with customer-controlled keys, and automated systems return limited safety signals without staff access to prompts or responses.[2] The design matters for regulated industrial agents, though technical assurance and independent audit evidence remain outstanding.
  3. Retrieval is becoming an agentic control layer. Mistral introduced a multi-step enterprise retrieval loop with search, open, navigate, read, and grep operations. It reports large gains on FinanceBench and OfficeQA Pro, alongside lower latency and token use.[3] These are vendor-reported benchmark results; the strategically important point is the architectural shift from static top-k retrieval toward inspect-and-verify behavior.
  4. Model economics continue to compress. OpenAI reduced GPT-5.6 Sol API and credit pricing by more than 20% for three months.[4] A temporary discount is not a new cost curve, but it strengthens the case for measuring cost per successfully completed workflow rather than cost per token.
  5. AI infrastructure commitments reached industrial-system scale. NVIDIA, SB Energy, and OpenAI announced an Ohio campus structured around an initial 4.25 IT-GW, with an option for another 3.75 IT-GW and a 20-year lease to OpenAI; NVIDIA also announced $1.5 billion of investment in SB Energy.[5] This is contracted and financed intent, not delivered capacity. Power interconnection, construction, hardware deployment, utilization, and community effects remain the milestones that matter.
  6. Humanoid robotics entered a commercial-reality phase. At the World Robot Conference, more than 300 companies were expected to show over 2,000 exhibits and launch more than 150 products. Yet Reuters found that broad adoption beyond pilots remains limited; an analyst estimated 50–70% of this year’s humanoid production may be used in “data factories,” and reported purchase costs remain above one cited two-year payback threshold.[6] The estimates are directional, not audited market statistics.
  7. Physical-AI data collection is becoming its own hardware category. Orbbec showed a robot-free multimodal data-collection system spanning first-person, handheld manipulation, wrist-level, and hub devices, alongside new robot-vision cameras.[7] The product claim is unverified, but the category is important: embodied intelligence needs scalable capture of real interactions, not only more robot hours.
  8. Simulation is moving toward closed-loop evaluation. NVIDIA’s Actuate 26 program emphasized world models, synthetic environment augmentation, neural simulation, fleet testing, data pipelines, and production reliability.[8] This was an industry event, not evidence of a new performance milestone; it is nevertheless a strong signal about where developer attention is concentrating.
  9. Quantum scale-up advanced at the refrigeration layer. IBM reported a demonstration of two coupled modular cryogenic cells intended eventually to support multi-chip systems and thousands of qubits per cryostat.[9] This validates a mechanical and cryogenic architecture, not useful fault-tolerant computation or inter-processor quantum advantage.
  10. Applied AI is being tested as a coordinated operational system. Google and the UK government launched a 30-month contrail-avoidance program in Shanwick airspace, including two roughly four-month operational trials.[10] Its value lies in linking prediction, air-traffic coordination, measurement, and policy—not in a model benchmark.

Bottom line for IPAI: prioritize evaluation and operational assurance over generic model access. The highest-leverage near-term assets are a privacy-preserving agent-safety testbed, a grounded-retrieval evaluation track, a physical-AI proving-ground scorecard, and a real-to-sim data-governance framework.

2. IPAI impact and relevance

Priority actions

  1. P1 — Establish an agent security and containment testbed. Within 30 days, define experiments for tool-capable agents covering network segmentation, least privilege, stop compliance, auditability, data exfiltration, destructive-action prevention, and incident pause procedures. Use OpenAI’s disclosed control stack as a hypothesis set, not as a standard: isolation, staged monitoring, automated investigation, and human escalation.[1] Success measure: reproducible attack scenarios, false-positive/false-negative rates, time to containment, and monitoring cost.
  2. P1 — Build a privacy-preserving longitudinal-safety demonstrator. Compare stateless moderation with cross-session risk detection under zero-retention constraints. Require customer-controlled keys or customer-controlled storage, minimized safety signals, explicit retention boundaries, and a threat model for metadata leakage.[2] Decision gate: do not represent the design as privacy-preserving until cryptographic, access-control, and operational claims can be independently tested.
  3. P1 — Create an evidence-grounded retrieval benchmark for industrial documents. Test static retrieval against agentic navigation on long manuals, maintenance records, standards, and multi-document engineering questions. Measure answer correctness, citation precision, unsupported-claim rate, retrieval coverage, latency, token use, and permission-boundary compliance. Mistral’s results provide a benchmark target, not a verified baseline.[3]
  4. P1 — Make commercial usefulness the physical-AI proving-ground criterion. Define task suites around productive work rather than locomotion spectacle: throughput, intervention frequency, recovery, safety events, changeover time, energy use, maintenance, and fully loaded cost per successful task. The WRC evidence suggests this is the market’s missing common language.[6][11]
  5. P2 — Launch a real-to-sim data-governance workstream. Treat wearable, handheld, wrist-mounted, teleoperated, fleet, and synthetic data as one governed pipeline. Specify consent, worker privacy, provenance, calibration, temporal synchronization, failure labels, domain coverage, and rights to derived models. New capture products and developer programs both point toward this convergence.[7][8]
  6. P2 — Use cost per verified outcome for model procurement. Re-run representative IPAI workflows during temporary frontier-model discounts, but record total agent turns, tool calls, human correction, latency, and failure costs. A lower token price matters only if task success and operating controls remain comparable.[4]
  7. P2 — Convene a European AI-infrastructure scenario exercise. Use the announced 8 IT-GW Ohio campus as a stress case for grid access, supply-chain concentration, financing, heat and water, workforce, sovereignty, and regional benefits.[5] Distinguish reserved land-power-shell capacity from installed accelerators and productive compute.
  8. P2 — Develop an applied-AI operational-trial template. Abstract the contrail program into a reusable pattern: model prediction, operational decision rights, safety constraints, counterfactual measurement, independent verification, and scale-up gates.[10] Candidate IPAI applications include industrial energy optimization, inspection, logistics, and predictive maintenance.
  9. P3 — Maintain a quantum infrastructure watch, not a procurement initiative. Track modular cryogenics, interconnect fidelity, logical-qubit roadmaps, serviceability, and error-correction overhead. IBM’s announcement is relevant to systems engineering but does not change near-term enterprise readiness.[9]

3. Artificial intelligence

3.1 Frontier capability triggered an operational pause

Fact: On 18 August, OpenAI said preliminary evidence suggested its forthcoming Astra model might meet the Critical cybersecurity threshold in its Preparedness Framework. It disclosed a two-week pause in RL training for deployable models; its largest planned frontier RL run remained on hold while smaller runs and evaluations continued. Workloads able to execute code or access networks were subjected to tighter sandboxing, network isolation, logging, and continuous testing.[1]

The disclosed monitoring design begins with activation classifiers at sampled tokens and escalates signals to higher-compute automated investigators. OpenAI said the system examines tool actions and available reasoning for unauthorized access, theft, destruction, and safeguard evasion; it targets a 30-minute alert-and-pause process and currently estimates monitoring overhead around 20% of monitored inference compute.[1]

Interpretation: This is an important governance milestone because a capability threshold reportedly changed the training schedule and research infrastructure, rather than merely generating a paper or policy statement. The key reusable mechanism is the coupling of capability evaluation to enforceable compute, network, and human-escalation controls.

Uncertainty: OpenAI has not yet published the promised technical incident report, independent replication of Astra’s capability assessment, classifier performance, or full evidence for the 20% overhead estimate. “May meet” is not the same as a confirmed Critical capability.

3.2 Zero-retention safety moves beyond single requests

Fact: On 19 August, OpenAI expanded eligible API access to Zero Data Retention for frontier models and previewed Private Safety Processing. In ZDR, prompts and responses are not retained after processing and are not available to OpenAI personnel; enterprise data is not used for training without opt-in. The new design aims to detect patterns across related interactions while underlying content stays on customer-controlled infrastructure or in OpenAI storage encrypted with customer-controlled keys.[2]

Interpretation: Agent safety and privacy are often treated as a trade-off: retaining sequences helps detect misuse, while deleting them limits exposure. The proposed architecture tries to separate the safety signal from content access. If independently validated, that pattern could matter for industrial agents handling trade secrets, health data, critical-infrastructure records, or public-sector material.

Uncertainty: The announcement does not provide a formal privacy proof, third-party audit, false-positive data, cross-account linking details, or a complete account of metadata. IPAI should therefore frame the approach as an auditable design hypothesis.

3.3 Enterprise retrieval becomes iterative and inspectable

Fact: Mistral’s 20 August Agentic Search release adds a multi-step retrieval loop over existing indexes, using search, open, navigate, read, and grep operations. Mistral reports FinanceBench correctness rising from 26.7% to 86%, OfficeQA Pro from 6.3% to 51.9%, p90 latency reductions of as much as 39.6%, and token reductions of up to one-third.[3]

Interpretation: The durable development is not the precise benchmark delta but the retrieval abstraction: an agent can inspect document structure, follow evidence, and verify an answer rather than rely on a one-shot chunk ranking. This can improve traceability and permission-aware search if the tools expose explicit access and provenance.

Uncertainty: The benchmark setup, model dependence, comparison configuration, and performance on noisy proprietary corpora need independent testing. More steps can also create new failure modes: navigation loops, permission leakage, citation laundering, and excessive latency.

3.4 Frontier inference became temporarily cheaper

Fact: OpenAI updated its GPT-5.6 page on 21 August to state that Sol API and credit prices were reduced by more than 20% for the following three months.[4]

Interpretation: Temporary pricing can accelerate experimentation and influence benchmark economics, but it should not anchor long-lived business cases. Organizations should calculate cost per accepted outcome, including tools, retries, monitoring, review, and fallback models. The larger market signal is continuing competition on useful-work economics, not only benchmark capability.

3.5 Compute supply is becoming a vertically integrated contract

Fact: NVIDIA said it would provide credit support for land, power, and shell capacity at SB Energy’s PORTS-Pike campus in Ohio. The initial design supports 4.25 IT-GW, with an option for another 3.75 IT-GW; OpenAI is named as customer under a 20-year lease. NVIDIA also announced a $1.5 billion investment in SB Energy and an initial $80 million community-benefits fund.[5]

Interpretation: AI competition is becoming vertical: developers are securing land, power, buildings, financing, chips, networking, and long-duration demand in one structure. “IT-GW” describes planned information-technology power capacity; it is not a measure of delivered tokens, useful compute, or grid-connected operation.

Uncertainty: The announcement did not establish a complete delivery schedule, actual accelerator mix, power sources, utilization, cooling performance, or final cost. Those milestones—not the headline capacity—will determine operational impact.

4. Embodied AI

4.1 The bottleneck moved from movement to experience

At the World Robot Conference, industry leaders repeatedly described real-world training data as the central constraint. ACE Robotics chairman Wang Xiaogang estimated the industry has accumulated roughly 100,000 hours and said ACE aims for tens of millions of hours within two years, using people equipped with lightweight sensors on production lines.[12] These numbers are company claims and should not be treated as a measured global inventory.

Interpretation: Human-carried sensing, teleoperation, deployed-fleet logs, and simulation are converging into an “experience supply chain.” The constraint is not raw hours alone. Coverage of rare states, action quality, sensor calibration, task outcomes, environment rights, worker consent, and failure annotation determine whether additional data improves behavior.

Prediction: Over the next 12–24 months, embodied-AI competition will increasingly be expressed in proprietary task datasets, data engines, evaluation environments, and integration partnerships—not only parameter counts or humanoid form factors. Confidence: medium.

4.2 “ChatGPT moment” forecasts remain forecasts

ACE’s chairman predicted a robot-brain breakthrough by the end of 2027, followed by another four to five years before broad commercial implementation. Unitree’s founder separately suggested a two-to-three-year earliest window.[12] These statements capture industry expectations but provide no falsifiable definition of a “ChatGPT moment.”

IPAI should replace the metaphor with thresholds: out-of-distribution task success, recovery without human takeover, safe transfer between bodies, learning efficiency, calibrated uncertainty, and economically competitive throughput. A breakthrough should be declared only when it generalizes across independent sites and operators.

5. Robotics

5.1 WRC exposed the commercialization gap

Fact: Beijing authorities expected more than 300 companies, over 2,000 exhibits, and more than 150 launches at WRC. Reuters reported that large-scale humanoid adoption beyond limited pilots remains uncommon. One analyst estimated 50–70% of 2026 humanoid production could go to data collection rather than paid work; reported robot prices of RMB 300,000–500,000 exceeded a cited RMB 160,000 two-year-payback target.[6]

Independent observation reinforced the reliability gap: AP reported a helper robot failing to fold a shirt after several minutes, even as the show featured sophisticated dancing, boxing, and interaction demonstrations.[11] A failed exhibition task is anecdotal, not a benchmark, but it usefully contrasts staged motion with unstructured manipulation.

Interpretation: The market is separating into three layers: reliable conventional automation; task-specific mobile or bimanual systems; and general-purpose humanoids still proving their economics. Wheeled and fixed-base designs may win many near-term deployments because they trade general mobility for stability, payload, runtime, and simpler safety cases.

5.2 Capital formation is ahead of general-purpose capability

Unitree announced its “Superman” high-speed robot before becoming the first general-purpose robotics company listed on mainland China’s stock market. Reuters reported a RMB 6.1 billion offering, retail oversubscription above 8,000 times, and a company-reported cumulative delivery of about 18,000 bipedal humanoids through July.[13]

Interpretation: Financing can expand manufacturing, component supply, data collection, and developer ecosystems. It does not establish task-level autonomy or customer returns. The 18,000 figure aggregates models and is company-reported; deliveries should not be equated with productive deployment.

5.3 Product diversity argues against a single winning morphology

WRC offerings ranged from industrial arms and quadrupeds to wheeled manipulators, tactile hands, reception humanoids, and emotionally expressive care robots. Reuters reported, for example, an eight-hour company-rated runtime for Galbot’s wheeled G1 and tactile sensing in Robotera’s dexterous hand, while conventional Siasun arms targeted heavy loading, welding, and assembly.[14]

Interpretation: IPAI’s test programs should be morphology-neutral and task-first. A humanoid should compete against a wheeled manipulator, cobot, industrial arm, process redesign, and human work—not only against another humanoid.

6. Sensing and perception

6.1 Robot vision expands into a data-acquisition stack

Fact: Orbbec debuted its Physis robot-vision line and fully showcased a robot-free data-collection platform with EGO first-person capture, UMI handheld manipulation capture, WristCam near-field capture, and a connection hub. The company positions the system for varied lighting, materials, occlusion, and long-tail tasks.[7]

Interpretation: This is less a camera story than a measurement-system story. Multiple synchronized perspectives can connect human demonstration, robot observations, and evaluation. The opportunity is to define interoperable schemas and calibration practices before vendor-specific data formats harden.

Uncertainty: The announcement provides no independent measures of depth accuracy, synchronization error, ruggedness, privacy controls, labeling quality, or downstream model improvement. “Human-like vision” is marketing language, not a validated capability.

6.2 Sensor fusion is now explicitly strategic

The new US National Security Science and Technology Strategy lists perception, recognition, and sensor fusion within AI and autonomy; it also identifies distributed apertures, data processing and fusion, adaptive optics, inertial sensors, atomic clocks, MEMS/NEMS, and integrated photonics.[15]

Interpretation: Sensing is increasingly inseparable from compute architecture, autonomy, and supply-chain policy. For IPAI, this supports a focus on calibrated multimodal perception and edge inference rather than treating sensors as commodity inputs.

7. Data generation and simulation

7.1 Real, synthetic, and operational data form one loop

NVIDIA’s Actuate 26 program framed world models as tools for physical reasoning, synthetic environment augmentation, policy evaluation through neural simulation, and real-time control. Other sessions focused on fleet testing, million-hour field datasets, MCAP pipelines, debugging, and production deployment.[8]

Fact versus signal: An agenda shows engineering priorities; it does not validate performance. Combined with WRC’s data-factory economics and Orbbec’s capture hardware, however, it provides triangulated evidence that the competitive unit is becoming the development loop: collect, curate, simulate, train, evaluate, deploy, observe, and repeat.

7.2 Closed-loop simulation needs validity evidence

Interpretation: Generated trajectories and neural simulations are most valuable for controlled rare-event exposure, rapid regression testing, and counterfactual analysis. They are dangerous when visually plausible outputs conceal incorrect dynamics or missing causal factors. IPAI should require three validity layers:

  1. Model validity: Does the simulator preserve task-relevant physics, contact, latency, and sensor noise?
  2. Policy validity: Do simulation rankings predict real-world policy rankings?
  3. Coverage validity: Does scenario generation expand the operational envelope without overrepresenting easy or synthetic-looking cases?

Recommended reporting should include real-to-sim and sim-to-real deltas, held-out-site performance, scenario provenance, and failure clusters—not a single aggregate success rate.

7.3 Human demonstration raises governance questions

Wearable and handheld collection can scale embodied data before robots are reliable enough to gather it themselves.[7][12] It also converts workplaces into data-generation environments. IPAI should anticipate consent, surveillance, biomechanical inference, re-identification, labor compensation, and rights over derivative models. These issues are not peripheral; they can determine whether a technically viable data engine is socially deployable.

8. Quantum technologies

8.1 IBM modularized the cryogenic system

Fact: IBM announced a modular cryogenic architecture and said it had demonstrated two coupled cells. The cells are intended to connect processors while allowing expansion and servicing, with a roadmap toward thousands of qubits per cryostat and IBM’s planned Starling fault-tolerant system in 2029.[9]

Interpretation: Quantum scale depends on classical wiring, cooling, packaging, control, calibration, and serviceability as much as on processor design. Modular cells could reduce the risk of scaling through one monolithic refrigerator and create clearer upgrade boundaries.

Uncertainty: The announcement establishes a cryogenic demonstration, not fault-tolerant operation. It does not show logical-qubit performance, useful algorithms distributed across processors, interconnect fidelity, error budgets, or an economic advantage.

8.2 Policy attention spans computing, sensing, networks, and migration security

The updated US critical-technology list includes quantum computing, sensing, communications and networking, plus enabling algorithms, materials, components, and fabrication. It separately identifies post-quantum cryptography.[15]

IPAI implication: Quantum monitoring should distinguish four timelines. Post-quantum migration is a current security program; quantum sensing may reach application niches before general computing; networking remains infrastructure research; and fault-tolerant computing is a longer-horizon capability program.

9. Other emerging technologies

9.1 AI-assisted contrail avoidance moves to airspace scale

Fact: Google and the UK government announced Operation Blue Skies on 18 August. The 30-month program will run two operational trials of roughly four months each in the eastern half of the North Atlantic’s Shanwick airspace. Google says that corridor accounts for approximately 5% of global contrail warming and describes the program as the first state-backed contrail-avoidance trial at oceanic-airspace scale.[10]

Interpretation: This is a strong model for applied AI because the technical artifact is embedded in a safety-critical operational system. Success depends on weather prediction, route optimization, air-traffic control, airline incentives, fuel trade-offs, and credible climate measurement. The trial’s value will be the verified system outcome, not prediction accuracy alone.

Uncertainty: No airspace-scale outcome is available yet. Claimed climate benefit, fuel penalty, dispatch reliability, false avoidance rate, and attribution methodology must be measured during the trials.

9.2 Critical-technology policy emphasizes convergence

The White House’s 20 August National Security Science and Technology Strategy updates its critical and emerging technology list. It places robotics, embodied intelligence, foundation and world models, multi-agent systems, interpretability, adversarial robustness, and privacy-preserving learning inside AI and autonomy. It also names digital twins, AI-enabled materials design, photonic and neuromorphic computing, post-quantum cryptography, integrated photonics, advanced sensing, quantum, biotechnology, nuclear energy, and space technologies.[15]

Interpretation: The list is a policy-priority signal, not funding or regulation by itself. Its most relevant feature is convergence: AI is treated simultaneously as a model technology, an autonomy layer, a scientific method, a cyber capability, and an infrastructure consumer. European institutions should expect research partnerships, procurement, export controls, and security requirements to reflect that wider frame.

10. Cross-domain synthesis

10.1 The frontier is relocating from artifacts to systems

Across domains, the most important objects were systems around the core technology: security controls around models, navigation around retrieval, power and financing around compute, data engines around robots, cryogenics around quantum processors, and air-traffic operations around climate prediction. This suggests a shift in institutional advantage from access to a component toward the ability to integrate, validate, and govern the complete loop.

10.2 Measurement infrastructure is becoming strategic infrastructure

Agent monitoring, evidence-aware retrieval, wearable robot-data capture, closed-loop simulation, and contrail verification all turn measurement into a control surface. Organizations that own task definitions, failure taxonomies, and audit evidence can steer development more effectively than organizations that only compare headline benchmarks.

10.3 Safety increasingly has an explicit resource cost

OpenAI’s company-estimated 20% monitoring overhead, secure workload migration, privacy-preserving processing, robot validation, and redundant simulation all consume compute, engineering time, and operational capacity.[1][2] Safety is therefore part of system economics, not a separate compliance line. IPAI should normalize reporting of assurance cost alongside performance and energy.

10.4 Physical AI’s scarce resource is governed experience

Humanoid production can grow faster than economically useful autonomy. Robots used mainly to collect data illustrate a transitional paradox: hardware is being deployed to manufacture the experience required to make future hardware useful.[6] The defensible asset is not volume alone but governed, diverse, outcome-linked experience.

10.5 Power, sovereignty, and security are now model variables

The Ohio compute contract and US technology strategy show that model capability cannot be separated from energy, capital, supply chains, geography, or national security.[5][15] IPAI’s technology analysis should therefore include deployment jurisdiction, infrastructure dependencies, and resilience in every major evaluation.

11. Watchlist

  1. OpenAI cyber-capability evidence: promised technical report, Astra threshold determination, resumption criteria for paused workloads, monitoring efficacy, and external review.[1]
  2. Private Safety Processing: cryptographic design, metadata handling, customer eligibility, false-positive rates, audit results, and availability beyond preview.[2]
  3. Agentic retrieval replication: independent FinanceBench and OfficeQA Pro results; performance on access-controlled, multilingual, scanned, and versioned industrial corpora.[3]
  4. GPT-5.6 pricing after the promotional period: effective cost per accepted workflow, discount expiration, and competitor response.[4]
  5. PORTS-Pike execution: permits, grid interconnection, construction milestones, installed hardware, energy and water sourcing, utilization, and community-benefit delivery.[5]
  6. Humanoid evidence: independent customer renewals, intervention rates, task throughput, lifetime cost, safety incidents, and the share of units doing paid work rather than data collection.[6]
  7. Embodied-data scaling: whether reported plans for tens of millions of hours materialize, how data quality is measured, and whether model gains transfer across sites and robot bodies.[12]
  8. Real-to-sim validity: public evidence that world-model or neural-simulation rankings predict real-world outcomes, especially for rare failures.[8]
  9. IBM modular cryogenics: processor interconnection results, thermal stability, wiring density, serviceability, logical-qubit milestones, and progress toward 2029.[9]
  10. Operation Blue Skies: trial start, avoided warming, fuel trade-off, forecast accuracy, operational burden, and independent methodology.[10]
  11. US critical-technology implementation: agency plans, appropriations, procurement criteria, international partnerships, and export-control consequences.[15]

12. Methodology and limitations

Research was bounded to 17–23 August 2026. Sources were selected for materiality, proximity to the event, and evidentiary quality. Primary company, government, and research sources establish what was announced; Reuters and AP provide independent observation and commercial context. Claims were cross-checked where a second source was available. Vendor benchmark results and forecasts remain attributed.

This is a high-signal review, not an exhaustive news index. Publication dates can lag underlying research, and some announcements extend earlier programs. Absence from the report does not imply absence of activity. Market estimates, product specifications, and deployment figures may use inconsistent definitions. No source was invented; each inline reference resolves to the numbered bibliography below.

Analytical standard: the report does not infer delivered capability from announced capacity, productive deployment from shipment, benchmark generality from a vendor-selected evaluation, or societal benefit from a planned trial.
